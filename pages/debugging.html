Debug

Debugging a Lisp machine

A CADR is debugged by another CADR. This page is how MIT did that, and how a board here does it.

The debugger is not a program on a host. It is a second Lisp machine, running bus cycles on the first over a cable of twenty-one wires, as though it were that machine’s own processor.

The program that drives the cable is CC, and it is Lisp software on the debugging machine.

Every board here is a debuggee the moment it is powered, which is what a CADR is with nothing set, and one of them can be told to be the debugger instead.

The long form of every figure on this page is docs/debug-cable.md.

What has run on a board

All three pieces have run on silicon: the window, the ribbon, and CC across the ribbon, both ways round. What has not crossed either is a write.

What How it was shown Shown
The machine halted, and its registers read CC through the register window, each register equal to the console’s reading of the same halt yes
The scratchpads CC’s reads over the window, equal to the console’s readout of the same words yes
Main memory, through the map Words read through the mapped window, each equal to the console’s reading of the same address; a write through the map moved MD and spent no microcycle yes
The ribbon between two boards The role taken and given back on a real cable between two Pmod JA connectors, the far end answering yes
A mirrored ribbon, found by the fabric The board told to connect reported the crossover it had found; forced the wrong way round, nothing answered yes
Two idle boards on that ribbon Both read a debuggee with nothing driving the connector; the role bit removed the old lock-out yes
Neither machine noticing Both boards ran Lisp throughout, at the rate they run at with no cable in them yes
CC over the ribbon Halted, read and started again, both ways round, every word equal to the far board’s own console and readout yes
Every microcycle accounted for The far machine’s cycle counter moved by one for each scratchpad read, and the debuggee’s own window saw no request at all yes
The guards, and the frames refused 0 refused in twenty-four readings, where the carrier before refused 163 and 185 in bursts; two frames on the other board are unaccounted for yes
A debug cycle over the carrier that runs now A status read typed at the debugger’s Listener, both ways round; no register of the far machine crossed yes
CC over the carrier that runs now What has crossed the guarded carrier is a status strobe not yet
A write over the ribbon None of CC’s writes has crossed a ribbon yet not yet

yes means a board itself has shown it, which is the same claim the colors on the board drawings make. Every reading, with its numbers, is in what two boards have shown and the debugger over the cable.
